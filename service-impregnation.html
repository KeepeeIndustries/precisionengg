Impregnation that turns porous castings into leak-proof parts

Sealing porosity in cast components — done in-house, right after machining, with records kept for every batch.

Overview

Porosity is normal. Leaks don't have to be.

Almost every casting has tiny pores. When oil, coolant, fuel or air is under pressure, connected pores become a leak path — and a leak means a rejected part or a warranty claim.

Impregnation fills those pores with sealant so the part becomes pressure-tight. Our plant is on site, right next to our machining cell, so parts don't travel to an outside vendor and back.

Process

How it works — in three steps

  1. Vacuum

    Parts go into a vacuum chamber. Air and loose particles are pulled out of the surface pores.

  2. Sealant fill

    Sealant is forced into the micro-pores under controlled pressure, filling the voids that cause leaks.

  3. Cure & wash

    The sealant is cured and excess is washed off. The part passes a final leak test before dispatch.

Why in-house matters

Faster, traceable and cheaper overall

No outsourcing delay

Parts stay on site. Lead time is predictable, with no third-party handling or re-inspection.

Process traceability

Batch, cycle settings and pressure data are logged against each part number.

Quality at source

Defective castings are caught before impregnation — not after shipment.

Lower total cost

No transport risk and no re-inspection cost means a lower landed cost per part.
