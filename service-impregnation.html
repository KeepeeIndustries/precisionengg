Vacuum impregnation that turns porous castings into sealed parts

Vacuum pressure impregnation (VPI) permanently seals micro-porosity in castings and powder-metal parts — recovering leak-test rejects and preventing field failures, with zero dimensional change.

Overview

Porosity is inevitable. Leaks are optional.

Every casting contains some micro-porosity. Under pressure — coolant, oil, fuel, air — interconnected porosity becomes a leak path, and a leak path becomes a warranty claim. Our vacuum pressure impregnation line seals that porosity permanently with methacrylate resin.

The process adds nothing to the part's dimensions and nothing to its weight that matters. Machined faces stay machined, threads stay clean, and components that failed leak test go back to the line as conforming parts.

Photo — impregnation autoclave and wash line

Process

How we run it

  1. Dry vacuum stage

    Parts are loaded and evacuated — air is pulled out of every interconnected pore before sealant is introduced.

  2. Sealant flood under vacuum

    Methacrylate sealant floods the chamber while vacuum holds, wicking into evacuated porosity.

  3. Pressure cycle

    Positive pressure drives sealant deep into the finest leak paths through-section.

  4. Centrifuge & wash

    Excess sealant is spun off and parts are washed — faces, bores and threads come out clean.

  5. Hot cure & leak test

    Sealant polymerises in a hot-water cure; parts are verified against your leak-test specification.

Applications

Where this process earns its keep

  • Cylinder heads
  • Pump & compressor bodies
  • Hydraulic valve blocks
  • Fuel system components
  • Transmission housings
  • Powder-metal parts
  • Die castings
  • Gravity castings

Benefits

What you get

  • Permanent seal — resin cures inside the porosity, not on the surface
  • Zero dimensional change — machined tolerances unaffected
  • Reject recovery — porous castings return as conforming parts
  • Pressure-tight performance — validated against your leak spec
  • Chemical & thermal resistance — stable across operating fluids
